Your house, simulated: 30 device types, one call each

Here is the classic testing gap: your QuickApp reacts to other devices — a scene fires on motion, a climate QA follows a temperature sensor, an alarm panel arms on a door sensor. To test that logic you need the devices around your QA. Until now you stubbed them by hand. Not anymore: flua ships simulated devices — 30 device types, bundled in the package (a pip install is enough), loaded with one call.

local temp = _FLUA.loadSimDevice("temperatureSensor")  -- returns the device id
fibaro.call(temp, "setValue", 21.5, "C")               -- the house warms up

The whole contract fits in one QA:

--%%name:HouseCheck
--%%type:com.fibaro.genericDevice
function QuickApp:onInit()
  -- the house: two devices, loaded by name — bundled with flua
  local temp = _FLUA.loadSimDevice("temperatureSensor")
  local motion = _FLUA.loadSimDevice("motionSensor")
  self:debug("sim devices:", temp, motion)

  -- a reacting QA: it watches the house like a real scene would
  local events = RefreshStateSubscriber()
  events:subscribe(
    function(event) return event.type == "DevicePropertyUpdatedEvent" end,
    function(event)
      self:debug("change:", json.encode(event.data))
    end)
  events:run()

  -- drive the devices — exactly like the HC3 UI or a scene would
  fibaro.call(temp, "setValue", 21.5, "C")
  fibaro.call(motion, "breach")
  fibaro.call(motion, "clearBreach")

  setTimeout(function()
    local dev = api.get("/devices/"..temp)
    self:debug("temp is now", dev.properties.value, dev.properties.unit)
    _FLUA.exit(0)   -- the sim devices stay up; end the run ourselves
  end, 300)
end

Run it and watch the subscriber follow every change — three DevicePropertyUpdatedEvents, exactly as a real device would post them. Your scenes and reacting QAs get the genuine event flow, offline, on your laptop.

One vocabulary for every type

The bundled set mirrors the QuickApp device templates — and every sim device answers a uniform, fibaro.call-able API:

  • Value sensors (temperature, humidity, light, rain, wind, energy, power, …): setValue(value, unit?) and setUnit(unit).
  • Breach sensors (motion, door, window, smoke, flood, fire, …): setValue(bool), breach(), clearBreach(). The sensor's value is the breach state — the HC3 contract, no extra property.
  • Switches and controllers: turnOn/turnOff/ setValue, plus their stock actions — arm/disarm/setArmed for the alarm partition, setThermostatMode/setHeatingThermostatSetpoint for the HVAC trio, setColor for the color controller, play/pause/setVolume for the player, emitCentralSceneEvent for the remote.

Unknown name? loadSimDevice throws an error that lists every bundled device — discoverability built in. And because the devices live inside flua, there is nothing to download, unpack, or configure.

They look real in the viewer too

Start any run with --ui and the sim devices appear as normal panels: ARMED/DISARMED on the alarm partition, colored TRUE/FALSE on breach sensors, live readings with their units on value sensors, the transport state on the player, the condition on the weather device. The types that have controls (switches, player, climate, color) can be driven straight from the panel while your QA reacts.

The weather, programmable

Want the house weather instead of one device? The weather simulator QA (weatherMgr) drives the simulated /weather endpoint — condition, temperature, humidity, wind — and every change posts a WeatherChangedEvent on the refreshStates feed. Combine it with the climate QA you are testing and you have a whole season on demand.

Sim devices keep the run alive for as long as they exist (no more setInterval(function() end, 3600000) hacks — that is _FLUA.keepAlive() now, and they call it themselves), so a test run ends with _FLUA.exit(0) when you say so.
